About Embergrid

Embergrid is an interactive archive for exploring where satellites detected fire activity and comparing the same season across years.

The project idea

MODIS and VIIRS can record different numbers of hotspots over the same area. Their sensors differ in resolution, sensitivity, and observation schedules. Embergrid explores how to compare older MODIS records with newer VIIRS observations so researchers can put an unusually active month in historical context.

How the fire calendar works

  1. Count occupied grid cells. Embergrid assigns NASA FIRMS detections to Indian districts. For each district, sensor, and month, it counts each grid cell with at least one detection once.
  2. Test the adjustment. Using years when both sensors operated, Embergrid tests whether adjusting MODIS improves district-level agreement with VIIRS. A separate validation year determines whether each district’s estimates can be shown.
  3. Show observations and label estimates. The calendar displays observed VIIRS counts where available and labels earlier MODIS-based estimates for districts that passed validation.

Explore the observations

The India Explorer maps individual satellite detections. The World Explorer combines monthly summaries from the published Fire Harmony dataset with archived detection points. Its global totals use Fire Harmony’s NOAA-20 VIIRS scale. The India calendar uses Embergrid’s district-level occupied-cell method.

Hotspot detections indicate where a sensor recorded a thermal anomaly. Burned area, fire cause, and usable satellite observation coverage require additional evidence.
