Before you explore

Frequently asked questions

A few things to know about the observations, estimates, and archive.

Read the project methods ↗
What does a hotspot represent?

A hotspot is a thermal anomaly recorded during a satellite overpass. Several detections can relate to the same event. Fire cause and burned area require additional evidence. Explore the original observations in the India Explorer.

Why compare MODIS and VIIRS?

MODIS provides an older record, while VIIRS detects fire activity at a finer resolution. Their counts can differ because of sensitivity and observation schedules. Embergrid tests adjustments to make earlier district-level MODIS activity more comparable with VIIRS. See how the calibration works.

Which years and places can I explore?

The India Explorer contains monthly detections from 2012–2025 across 36 states and union territories. The district calendar extends to 2003 using labeled estimates where validation passes. The World Explorer provides the published Fire Harmony series for countries and available subdivisions.

Are historical estimates shown for every district?

Earlier estimates appear when a district has sufficient paired training months and the selected adjustment beats raw MODIS in the 2023 validation check. The calendar labels those estimates and shows observed VIIRS from February 2012 onward. View a district’s results in the Fire Calendar.

Can I download the data I am viewing?

Yes. The India Explorer exports detections for the selected filters. The World Explorer offers monthly totals and a separate export of displayed points. World point queries display up to 8,000 records and report the total matched count and any missing partitions.

Does a zero count mean there was no fire?

Cloud, overpass timing, sensor sensitivity, and archive availability can affect detections. A zero count alone cannot establish the absence of burning. Usable observation coverage needs verification with satellite fire masks. Read the observation context.
